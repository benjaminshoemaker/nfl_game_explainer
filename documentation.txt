GAME ANALYSIS DASHBOARD - STAT DOCUMENTATION
============================================

This dashboard compares several descriptive factors to help explain a game's
result. The factors are not a validated prediction model.
The live dashboard displays eight rows: turnovers, success rate, adjusted
yards per play, explosive play rate, points per trip inside 40, average start
field position, penalty yards, and non-offensive points. Other metrics below are available in analysis
outputs but are not additional dashboard rows. ESPN is the reference for
official box-score statistics; explicitly noted derived metrics can differ.
For completed games, displayed full-game Total Yards and Turnovers follow ESPN's
box score (plus the documented onside-recovery addition). If available plays
do not reproduce those totals or ESPN's offensive-play count, a source-gap
notice appears. A matching yard total alone does not prove every snap is
present. Play-derived
factors and competitive-play splits can still be incomplete in that case.
The Competitive scope includes every play from the start of the game through
the latest play whose win probability is below the configured threshold at
the start or end of the play. This keeps the full lead-up to a competitive
game state in scope: if the game returns to the competitive range, previously
excluded plays are added back. Only the trailing stretch after the latest
competitive state is excluded. Plays with incomplete win-probability data are
kept in scope conservatively.
ESPN play text can use older team abbreviations (CLV, HST, WAS, ARZ, BLT,
LA, or JAC). Team IDs and one shared alias map align those names with the
box score. When accepted penalty yardage or team attribution is unknown,
known yards remain summed and the penalty-yard factor comparison is undecided.

FACTOR BAR SCALE
----------------

The winner's shaded value cell identifies the team leading a factor. The
adjacent bar shows the absolute team-to-team gap divided by the rounded
historical large-gap reference for that factor and selected scope, capped at a
full bar. A tie or unresolved comparison has no bar. Rate bars require 20
eligible offensive plays per team; points per trip requires two qualifying
trips per team; starting field position requires five drive starts per team.
This compares factor values, not causal impact or win-probability contribution.

The references come from completed regular-season games. They are also used
during live games, where stage-specific calibration remains provisional. See
FACTOR_BAR_CALIBRATION.md for values, sample, method, and limitations.

PLAY EPA
--------

Play EPA estimates how many expected points a play added for the team listed
on its card. It compares expected points before the play with points scored
or the next ESPN possession state. For kickoffs, the model starts from the
receiving team's perspective and the card reverses the sign to show the
listed kicking team's perspective. A live play can remain unavailable until
ESPN publishes a successor state. Plays with incomplete inputs or unsupported
overtime states remain unavailable; no zero is imputed. The expected-points
model is nflfastR's published model, with a separate 2025 field-goal baseline.

SPECIAL TEAMS METRICS
--------------------

"Net Punting" is the average punt distance after subtracting punt-return yards
and 20 yards for each touchback, divided by punts. Punt distances and return
yards are separate in ESPN's play text/stat fields; the play's statYardage alone
is not net punting.

Kickoffs do not have a directly comparable standard "net kickoff" average.
Analysis outputs report "Avg Opponent Kickoff Start": the average own-yardline
where the receiving team's possession begins after the kicking team's kickoffs.
This reflects the actual field-position result, including touchbacks and
enforced kickoff penalties. Lower opponent starting yardlines are better for
the kicking team. Kickoffs without a resulting opponent possession are omitted.


CORE FIVE FACTORS (from Bill Connelly's framework)
--------------------------------------------------

1. TURNOVERS
   
   What it measures: Each team's giveaways (interceptions, lost fumbles and
   muffed kicks), plus onside kicks recovered by the kicking team charged to
   the receiving team. The dashboard tooltip says "includes onside kick
   recoveries". ESPN's official turnover total excludes that onside addition.
   
   How to read it: Lower is better. The dashboard shows each team's count,
   not the difference; turnover margin is available separately in the analysis.
   
   Why it matters: Turnovers create possession asymmetry. If you turn it over
   twice and force none, your opponent gets 2 extra drives. Teams winning the
   turnover battle win approximately 73% of games.
   
   Note: Turnover on downs is NOT included here—see FAQ for rationale.


2. YARDS PER PLAY (Efficiency)
   
   What it measures: Two explicitly named values are available in analysis
   outputs. "Official Yards Per Play (Full Game)" is ESPN's box-score value.
   "Adjusted Yards Per Play" is displayed in the dashboard: this app's
   offensive yards divided by selected offensive plays (including aborted
   snaps, excluding kneels and spikes).
   The adjusted value can also reflect a competitive-play win-probability
   filter; the official value is shown only for the full game.
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. NFL average is approximately 5.0-5.5 yards
   per play.
   
   Why it matters: Captures overall offensive firepower in a single number.
   However, this can be skewed by a few explosive plays, so it's complemented
   by Success Rate.


3. SUCCESS RATE (Play Success %)
   
   What it measures: The percentage of plays that gain "enough" yards, defined as:
   - 1st down: 40% of yards needed (4+ yards for 1st & 10)
   - 2nd down: 60% of yards needed
   - 3rd/4th down: 100% of yards needed (convert or gain first down)
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. Elite offenses exceed 45%; struggling
   offenses fall below 35%.
   
   Why it matters: This is the single most predictive stat in football analytics.
   Unlike yards per play, it's not skewed by explosives—it measures whether a
   team consistently moves the chains. A team can have modest YPP but high
   Success Rate by avoiding negative plays.


4. EXPLOSIVE PLAYS (10+ Yard Runs, 20+ Yard Passes)
   
   What it measures: Raw count of runs gaining 10+ yards and passes gaining
   20+ yards.
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. This is a volume metric.
   
   Why it matters: Big plays create scoring opportunities and swing momentum.
   However, explosive plays are less predictable game-to-game than efficiency.


5. EXPLOSIVE PLAY RATE (Explosiveness)
   
   What it measures: Percentage of selected offensive plays that are explosive
   under the 10+ run / 20+ pass thresholds above.
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. Typically ranges from 8-15% for NFL teams.
   
   Why it matters: Normalizes explosive plays for pace. A team running 80 plays
   might have more explosives than a team running 55, but the rate tells you
   which team is more dangerous per snap.


6. POINTS PER TRIP INSIDE 40 (Finishing Drives)
   
   What it measures: Average points scored when a team's drive reaches the
   opponent's 40-yard line.
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. Maximum theoretical is 7.0 (TD every trip).
   Good teams score 4.5-5.5; poor finishers fall below 3.5.
   
   Why it matters: Getting into scoring position means nothing if you settle for
   field goals or turn it over. This captures red zone efficiency plus the
   ability to convert good field position into points.

   The dashboard also shows each qualifying trip's points (including zero-point
   trips), the trip count, and total points from those trips for the selected
   competitive or full-game scope. A touchdown that crosses the opponent's 40
   on the scoring play counts as a trip reaching the 40.


7. AVERAGE STARTING FIELD POSITION (Field Position)
   
   What it measures: The average yard line where a team begins its offensive
   drives. Expressed from the nearer goal line: "Own XX" on a team's half
   (e.g., "Own 28") or "Opp XX" on the opponent's half (e.g., "Opp 32").
   
   How to read it: A start closer to the opponent's goal is better. "Own 35"
   is better than "Own 22." "Opp 32" is better still: it is 68 yards from
   the offense's own goal line. On the opponent's half, smaller numbers are
   better ("Opp 20" is better than "Opp 32").
   
   Why it matters: Field position compounds over a game. Starting at your 35 vs
   your 20 on every drive is a 15-yard advantage per possession. Over 12 drives,
   that's 180 hidden yards. This captures punt/kick return effectiveness,
   turnover locations, and opponent punt distance.


ADDITIONAL FACTORS (addressing gaps in original framework)
----------------------------------------------------------

8. PENALTIES (Yards)
   
   What it measures: Total penalty yards assessed against each team.
   Full-game values use ESPN's box score. Competitive values sum accepted
   play-level penalties through the latest competitive play; all earlier
   penalties stay in scope even if their individual win probability was above
   the threshold. If the game returns to the competitive range, earlier
   penalties are added back. ESPN kickoff
   placement fouls (out of bounds or short of the landing zone) count as
   accepted penalties but charge zero penalty yards; their placement distance
   is not penalty yardage. If another accepted penalty lacks reliable yards or
   team attribution, known assessed yards remain summed for each team, but
   the factor comparison stays undecided. The drilldown identifies the
   unresolved play.
   
   How to read it: Lower is better. This is inverted from most stats.
   
   Why it matters: Penalties are invisible in efficiency stats but can be
   game-deciding. A 15-yard personal foul that extends a drive, or a holding
   call that negates a touchdown, directly impacts scoring in ways no other
   stat captures.


9. NON-OFFENSIVE POINTS (Def/ST Scores)
   
   What it measures: Points scored by defense or special teams, including:
   - Pick-sixes
   - Fumble return touchdowns
   - Punt/kick return touchdowns
   - Blocked kick return touchdowns
   - Safeties
   
   How to read it: Higher is better. Most games this is 0-0. When it's not,
   it's often decisive.
   
   Why it matters: A pick-6 is worth ~10+ points of swing (7 points scored plus
   the possession the opponent lost). In the base Turnovers stat, it only shows
   as +1. Non-offensive scores are discrete, high-impact events that need
   separate tracking.


10. POSSESSIONS (Drive Count)
    
    What it measures: Total number of offensive drives for each team.
    
    How to read it: Context-dependent. More possessions means more opportunities,
    but can also indicate quick drives (good if scoring, bad if punting).
    
    Why it matters: Two teams can have identical efficiency, but if one team had
    12 possessions and the other had 9, volume explains the scoring gap. Possession
    count also captures pace of play and time of possession indirectly.


INTERPRETING THE DASHBOARD
--------------------------

Factor Count: The summary shows how many displayed factors each team "won."
The count is descriptive; it is not a calibrated prediction of the winner.

Strength Indicators: The diamond symbols show magnitude of advantage:
- ◆◆◆ Dominant (>30% difference)
- ◆◆ Strong (15-30% difference)
- ◆ Slight (5-15% difference)
- · Minimal (<5% difference)

A team can win fewer factors but still win the game if they dominated the factors
they won (e.g., winning Turnovers +3 matters more than losing Explosiveness by 1%).

When Factors Conflict: If the stats suggest Team A should have won but Team B
actually won, look for:
- Non-offensive points (a pick-6 can overcome losing most factors)
- Finishing drives (getting to the 40 means nothing without points)
- Turnovers in critical field position (not all turnovers are equal)


TEAM-SEASON AGGREGATION (DESCRIPTIVE, VERSION 1)
----------------------------------------------

The season layer pools exact game-parser counters before display rounding.
Success rate is total successful plays / selected offensive plays. Adjusted
yards per play is total selected offensive yards / selected offensive plays.
Explosive rate is total selected explosives / selected offensive plays.
Points per trip pools points and qualifying trips inside the opponent's 40.
Starting field position pools start coordinates (0 at own goal, 100 at the
opponent's goal) and counted drive starts. These are ratios of sums, never
unweighted averages of game percentages. No-opportunity ratios are unavailable,
not zero. Scrambles and sacks retain the canonical pass-dropback treatment.

Turnovers, penalty yards, and non-offensive points are displayed per covered
game, with total numerators preserved. Turnovers retain the dashboard's onside
recovery addition. Full-game giveaways and penalties use official box-score
totals; other inputs come from the canonical play parser. Historical source
or convention disagreements are not silently repaired from another provider.

Own means the listed team's output; opponent means output by its opponents in
those games. Opponent penalty yards are not a defensive-skill measurement.
For success, yards/play, explosives, finishing, field position, and non-offensive
points, net = own minus opponent. For turnovers and penalties, net = opponent
minus own. Positive net therefore always favors the listed team. Both sides of
net use the same covered games, each with its own opportunity denominator;
paired values are retained to make partial-coverage calculations inspectable.

The expected-game denominator comes from the season/type/week-filtered schedule,
not the number of downloaded payloads. Records come from completed-game scores.
Missing payloads and source gaps remain visible. The initial conservative
screen excludes play-derived factors from a game if either team's plays/yards/
turnovers fail box-score reconciliation. Missing validation fields, penalty
totals, or scoring sequences also affect the relevant coverage. Independent
efficiency audits can add exclusions, but do not silently clear source gaps.
Passing these screens is not proof that every metric is correct.

Only complete, nonempty team factors receive ranks. Rankings use unrounded
values and competition ties (1, 1, 3); tied percentiles use the midpoint.
Every rank publishes its qualified pool size and league size. Incomplete
covered-sample values remain available without a rank. The league comparison
is the pooled value over explicitly reported covered team-games.

Weekly snapshots exclude later weeks, preseason, and playoffs by explicit
season and season-type keys. They are retrospective recalculations, not
historically published forecasts. Full-game aggregation does not use pregame
WP, betting odds, or external power ratings. Power-rating weights, opponent
adjustments, prior-season blending, and accuracy claims remain separate model
research; these descriptive ranks must not be labeled predictive strength.

NFLVERSE SEASON ADAPTER (nflverse-eight-factors-v1)
The season research source is nflverse. Its explicit input whitelist excludes
odds, WP, EPA, and imported ratings. Only completed regular-season games enter
snapshots. Source checksums and calculation versions are recorded; snapshots
are retrospective recalculations, not historical pregame publication archives.
Success retains the app's 40/60/100 distance definition, not nflverse success.
Run/pass plays exclude deleted plays and conversion attempts. Scrambles and
sacks use the 20-yard dropback explosive threshold; runs use 10 yards.
Lateral receptions use provider passing yards when available, consistent with
the independent audit. Rates pool unrounded counters over opportunities.
Drive starts use consistent scrimmage-row drive_start_yard_line metadata;
kickoff-row metadata is excluded because it can disagree with possession start.
A long offensive touchdown qualifies as a trip inside the 40. Its conversion
belongs to the same drive. Return touchdowns and their conversions, safeties,
and defensive conversion returns count as non-offensive points.
Lost fumbles are charged to the fumbling team, including return fumbles.
Successful onside recoveries count against the receiving team. Conversion
turnovers are excluded. Ambiguous multiple-fumble attribution remains missing.
Penalties sum the provider's attributed play-level penalty yards, including
no-play fouls; this is not asserted to equal official multi-foul boxscore totals.
Scoring events must reconcile to scheduled final scores. A discrepancy withholds
scoring-related factors only. Missing/conflicting drive starts withhold starting
field position. Incomplete factors retain partial values but receive no rank.
Own and opponent rates use their own opportunities; net pools matched games,
with subtraction reversed for turnovers and penalties so positive is better.
2024 and 2026 Week 3 have complete eight-factor coverage. 2025 retains source
gaps for a conflicting CAR drive start against NO in Week 10 and ambiguous
multiple-fumble attribution in SEA-CAR Week 17. No imputation is applied.

EXPERIMENTAL PREDICTIVE MODEL V1
2011 initializes prior effects; 2012–2021 fits coefficients and standardization;
2022–2023 selects hyperparameters by scoring-margin RMSE; 2024–2025 is the frozen
first evaluation. Predictors for week W use only weeks < W, including when a
week spans calendar years. End-of-season corrected data is retrospective, not
historically as-published. The test years are now inspected; subsequent tuning
must not describe those same years as a fresh untouched test.
For each factor, weighted ridge estimates team production and opponent allowed
effects jointly: observed rate = current league mean + own effect + allowed
effect. Weights are opportunities divided by average team-game opportunities.
Two neutral team-games regularize each effect; 0, 2, or 4 additional prior-games
pull toward half of the preceding season's effect. Effects are centered, and
net = production minus allowed (reversed for turnovers/penalties). Missing
observations are excluded from that factor's fit, not entered as observed zeros.
A standardized ridge regression predicts home-minus-away margin from the eight
home-minus-away net factors. Intercept learns average home advantage. Candidate
penalties are 10, 100, 1000. A separate logistic fit uses the same setting;
probability metrics exclude ties and condition on decisive games. This first
round selects for margin error, not independently optimized probabilities.
Unadjusted eight-factor, raw point differential, opponent-adjusted point
differential, and home-only models provide baselines. A paired season-week
block bootstrap estimates uncertainty in test RMSE differences (2000 draws).
It is not a guarantee across future seasons. Correlated factor contributions
are additive predictive explanations, not causal effects or universal weights.
Experimental team rating = sum of coefficient times adjusted net factor,
expressed as points above the league average. Current exports retain training
through 2021 and are research-only. No FPI/DVOA/Elo/EPA benchmark is claimed.

GAME-PAGE HIGHLIGHTS (EXPERIMENTAL)
The three game-page highlights are selected dynamically from available factor
comparisons. The first version ranks the observed game gap using the frozen
experimental season-prediction coefficient for that factor, favors factors
with contributing-event evidence, and discounts a second highlight from a
closely related factor group. A factor must pass the dashboard's comparison
and sample checks. These coefficients predict future scoring margin from
season-level team data; they are a review heuristic for completed-game
highlights, not validated same-game importance weights or causal estimates.

PREDICTIVE EXPERIMENT V2 (RESEARCH ONLY)
The second experiment retrains coefficients annually from 2012 through the
preceding season, with 2011 initializing priors. Selection pools chronological
validation forecasts for 2018–2023. The same settings then produce 2024–2025
follow-up forecasts; those years were inspected in v1 and are not fresh holdouts.
For 2025 forecasts, coefficient fitting may use 2024 outcomes, but settings and
factor selection remain fixed from the pre-2024 validation procedure.
Phase 1 tests all one-, two-, and three-factor subsets, each leave-one-out
seven-factor subset, all eight, separate production/prevention for all eight,
and point differential plus each one-/two-/three-factor subset or all eight.
Point differential alone is the comparison baseline. Ridge penalties are
10/100/1000. Selection is pooled validation scoring-margin RMSE.
Phase 2 keeps each family's selected columns, testing 2/4/8 prior pseudo-games
and two neutral regularization profiles. Uniform adds two neutral pseudo-games
per factor. Factor-specific uses [2,2,2,6,8,2,6,8] in canonical factor order;
this is a predefined hypothesis, not measured reliability. Previous-season
effects are first halved. Missing observations are excluded, not observed zeros.
Phase 3 keeps the selected stabilization and tests no decay, 4-week, or 8-week
half-life. Age is cutoffWeek - 1 - observationWeek; only strictly earlier weeks
are eligible. Age weights multiply opportunity weights without renormalizing
away the loss of effective sample size. Priors remain fixed during a season.
Point differential receives its own validation-selected prior strength and
recency, using two neutral pseudo-games. Its venue treatment remains the same
home-designation-only approximation as v1; neutral sites are not identified.
Production and prevention separately sum exactly to the original net factors.
Their downstream weights can differ in the separate-sides family. All feature
scaling is fitted only on each fold's training seasons. Logistic C is selected
separately from [0.001,0.01,0.1,1] by validation log loss after margin-based feature
selection; tied games remain excluded from conditional winner probabilities.
Exploratory paired week-block RMSE intervals do not account for the whole model
search. No future superiority, causal explanation, or FPI/DVOA claim follows.
The first-round artifacts and game-highlight weighting remain unchanged.

STRONGER POINT-DIFFERENTIAL COMPARATOR AUDIT
research/team_power_baseline_audit.py estimates team strengths jointly with a
home-advantage coefficient. Neutral sites have venue indicator zero. Home
advantage has 16 pseudo-games toward the previous season estimate, initialized
at 2.5 points. Team effects shrink toward half previous-season strength with
2/8/32 prior games plus two neutral games; recency is none/4/8 weeks. Downstream
ridge uses strength difference and venue times fitted home advantage, optionally
rest difference, with no intercept to preserve neutral-site symmetry. Training
uses previous seasons; settings selection uses previous three validation seasons.
The previously selected eight-factor candidate is unchanged during this audit.
Results remain exploratory and comparator sensitivity prevents a robust claim
of superiority. The original v1-v4 outputs are preserved.

SITUATION AND QB-HISTORY RESEARCH
Round six adds early-down efficiency (downs 1–2), close-state efficiency (first
three quarters and pre-play absolute score differential <=14), and close-state
pass/rush splits, preserving dropback classification for sacks and scrambles.
These are situation-specific rates, not an expected-points adjustment. They use
strictly earlier weeks, opponent adjustment, and the existing stabilized rates.
QB-history features use the dominant identified dropback player from the team's
last observed game, his share of team dropbacks, whether that identity changed
from the preceding game, and that player's prior observed yards/dropback,
interception rate and game count from current/prior two seasons. Source rows
use passer_player_id, or rusher_player_id on scrambles. No actual next-game QB
field is consumed. All same-week forecasts precede all same-week updates.
Home-minus-away feature differences include missing-history flags. The proxy can
be stale after offseason changes or injuries; it must never be described as a
confirmed projected starter. No production UI or headline weights change.

EXPERIMENTAL TEAM POWER RATING (review prototype)
The frozen ridge model in research/models/frozen-margin-model.json predicts an
upcoming regular-season game's home-minus-away scoring margin, in points. It
was fitted on 2012–2025 and must not be used to present earlier-season ratings
as forecasts. Season performance and predictive strength are separate outputs.

research/team_power_ratings.py computes the original p8/h8 adjusted factor and
point-margin features, plus previous observed quarterback usage. Team ratings
sum centered feature contributions, excluding rest, neutral-site and intercept
terms. This is the antisymmetric neutral strength component: averaging the
margin over both home/away designations removes any designation offset. A +6
team against a +2 team has a +4 neutral strength difference. The original
frozen matchup formula retains its learned intercept and schedule terms.
Historical feature estimates are not refitted to remove venue exposure.

Each factor contribution combines its net, own and prevention terms; all ten
component contributions (eight factors, scoring margin and QB history) sum to
the total before rounding. These are predictive associations, not causal
explanations. Rankings use unrounded strength, with competition ties.

The review's 2026 snapshot is through Week 3, generated retrospectively on
October 5. Refreshed QB source files may contain later games, but only game IDs
in the completed-week input snapshot are admitted. Source hashes are recorded.
There is no confirmed-next-starter input, final-record projection, playoff
forecast or prospective prediction archive. The 2018–2025 research results
measure annual model selection/refitting, not this final frozen vector on all
historical years. They do not establish prospective or FPI/DVOA superiority.

TEAM POWER COMPONENT CONTEXT
The team power table ranks each of the ten grouped model contributions across
the current snapshot's 32 teams. Higher predicted-point contribution is better.
Competition ranks preserve ties; radar percentiles use tied midranks scaled
from 0 (last) to 100 (first), with 50 for an all-tied component. These can differ
from raw descriptive factor ranks because opponent adjustment, shrinkage,
recency and grouped coefficients intervene.

The radar compares the selected team to the actual teams in overall positions
16 and 1, not a synthetic per-component median or maximum. Duplicate teams are
drawn once, with their roles combined in the legend. Under an exact overall
rating tie, stable team-ID ordering determines those benchmark positions.

Standardized model weights are computed from the frozen model's checksum-
verified original training matrix, restricted to its 2012–2025 fit seasons.
For each component, sum coefficient-times-feature terms within each historical
matchup, compute the population standard deviation across those matchups, and
normalize component SDs to sum to 100%. Grouping before measuring SD respects
within-component covariance and net/own/prevention redundancy. Weights are
common to every team. They are descriptive effect-size shares, NOT literal
percentage coefficients, variance attribution, causal importance or measured
incremental predictive value. Expandable details expose every raw coefficient.
The frozen model, team ratings and prediction formula are unchanged.

POWER RATING CALCULATION WALKTHROUGH
The team page shows observed eight-factor own/opponent/net values, then adds
raw scoring margin and all six previous-QB inputs. It compares two fits of the
current-season data: the normal joint opponent fit and a diagnostic fit with
opponent coupling terms removed. Both use identical observed games, real
historical priors, opportunity weights, recency decay and penalties. The prior
already contains historical opponent adjustment. This is a controlled current-
schedule comparison, not a fully unadjusted historical model or an alternate
prediction used in rankings. The production/default ratings calculation is
unchanged; adjust_opponents=False is used only for this diagnostic.

The diagnostic removes factor own/opponent cross-entries from the normal
matrix and point-rating team-pair cross-entries, retaining diagonals and RHS.
Both estimates are centered with the same convention. Factor comparison rows
show net advantage (own plus prevention), in native units relative to average;
these are not absolute adjusted rates. QB inputs are not opponent-adjusted.
The actual calculation exposes every model input, league mean, centered input,
raw coefficient and their product. Products sum to grouped contributions, and
the eight-factor subtotal plus scoring margin plus QB contribution equals the
team rating. Display rounding can prevent visible rounded cells summing exactly.

OPPONENT-ADJUSTMENT IMPACT IN POINTS
The explainer before Step 2 applies the exact frozen formula to both the actual
feature vectors and the opponent-off diagnostic vectors. Only the first 25
factor/scoring features differ; QB and context inputs are identical. Each fit
is centered over the same league. Actual rating minus diagnostic rating is the
current-season opponent-adjustment impact, in rating points. Component deltas
sum to the total; the league-average impact is zero up to floating-point error.

Rank impacts from largest upward change to largest downward change, preserving
competition ties and tied-midrank percentiles. This is an adjustment-impact
ranking, NOT an independent strength-of-schedule ranking: it depends on the
factor-specific fit, regularization and the learned coefficients. The actual
historical prior remains fixed in both calculations. This delta is already
included in the published rating and must never be added again. The explainer
also shows the league median/range, all-team comparison, and component deltas.

its design, current-data league baseline, opportunity weights, decay and

the prior contribution. This is an exact additive model decomposition, not a
percentage attribution of accuracy or literal causal influence. QB history

WEEKLY POWER-RATING PUBLICATION AND MARKET COMPARISON
The frozen 2026 formula predicts upcoming home-minus-away scoring margin using
completed prior-week factor, scoring, schedule-context and QB-history inputs.
The fixed point-only comparator uses original_p8_h8_point_margin, Ridge alpha 10,
fit on 2012–2025; intercept 2.0812818964392195 and coefficient 1.4505874207360845.
The weekly updater does not fit weights or use odds as model inputs. The source
schedule may contain odds, but prediction features are explicitly whitelisted.
Publication saves the full named feature vector, both predicted margins, actual
publication timestamp, frozen model hashes, input copies, and executable code.
A missing entire game or scoring failure blocks publication. Existing factor-level
quality exclusions remain in force and are shown; they are never zero imputation.
Only first sealed pregame forecasts enter the prospective ledger. A historical
replay uses strictly earlier-week observations but is labeled retrospective because
the source snapshot and publication were obtained after the target games.
Evaluation compares the same completed games with finite closing lines for all
three predictors. nflverse spread_line is the expected home-minus-away margin
(positive means home favored). MAE averages absolute scoring-margin errors; RMSE
is the square root of mean squared errors. Winner accuracy excludes actual ties;
